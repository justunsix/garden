### Process to a Home

3 homeowner criteria

1.  In Need of housing
2.  Financial situation
3.  Willing to partner with H4H

### [Drywall](http://en.wikipedia.md/wiki/Drywall)

- To cut, score with exacto knife, knee/punch wall from behind, then
  score from behind to remove piece
- For large cuts or small drawn cuts, use a small saw (do not use large
  saw)

### Knotting Extension cords

- **Do not knot normal electrical power cords.** Only knot joints
  between extension cords to prevent cords from unplugging themselves
  during use. See picture below.

Source: [Knot Your Power Cords Like a Carpenter to Avoid Unwanted
Unplugging \|

### Drywall cont'd

- Walls and homes settle over one year or due to vibrations. This
  settling may cause wall cracking or frame screw marks to appear
- Wall/ceiling is drywall and is constructed top to bottom 1.
  ceiling, 2. top wall, 3. bottom wall

### Miter saw use

1.  Take off all gloves.
2.  Measure wood, measure to center of attaching piece.
3.  Use speed square for marking
4.  Eye ball cut first, check block
5.  Plug in with knot in cord if extension
6.  Check cut again and press with trigger to speed up saw to full
    throttle before the wood is cut.
7.  Bring saw into the wood at full speed.

### Circular Saw

1.  Use chalking (string with chalk like a tape measure) for
    measurement.
2.  Pinch tape to make a line

### Level (tool)

- Check straight surfaces.
- "Make it plum" = make it straight

### Drywall Placement

Source: [Drywall - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drywall)

> As an alternative to a week-long plaster application, an entire house
> can be drywalled in one or two days by two experienced
> [drywallers](http://en.wikipedia.md/wiki/Drywall_mechanic), and
> drywall is easy enough to use that it can be installed by many amateur
> home carpenters. In large-scale commercial construction, the work of
> installing and finishing drywall is often split between the drywall
> mechanics, or *hangers*, who install the wallboard, and the *tapers*
> and *mudmen*, or *float crew*, who finish the joints and cover the
> nailheads with [drywall
> compound](http://en.wikipedia.md/wiki/Drywall_compound).\[[*citation
> needed*](http://en.wikipedia.md/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed)\]
>
> Drywall is cut to size, using a large
> [T-square](http://en.wikipedia.md/wiki/T-square), by scoring the paper
> on the front side (usually white) with a [utility
> knife](http://en.wikipedia.md/wiki/Utility_knife), breaking the sheet
> along the cut, scoring the paper backing, and finally breaking the
> sheet in the opposite direction. Small features such as holes for
> outlets and light switches are usually cut using a [keyhole
> saw](http://en.wikipedia.md/wiki/Keyhole_saw) or a small high-speed
> bit in a rotary tool. Drywall is then fixed to the
> [wall](http://en.wikipedia.md/wiki/Wall) structure with
> [nails](http://en.wikipedia.md/wiki/Nail_(engineering)), glue, or more
> commonly in recent years, the now-ubiquitous *drywall
> [screws](http://en.wikipedia.md/wiki/Screw)*.

> After the sheets are secured to the [wall
> studs](http://en.wikipedia.md/wiki/Platform_Framing_Method) or ceiling
> [joists](http://en.wikipedia.md/wiki/Joist), the seams between drywall
> sheets are concealed using *joint tape* and several layers of *joint
> compound* (sometimes called *mud*). This compound is also applied to
> any screw holes or defects. The compound is allowed to air dry then
> typically sanded smooth before painting. Alternatively, for a better
> finish, the entire wall may be given a *skim coat*, a thin layer
> (about 1 mm or 1/16 inch) of finishing compound, to minimize the
> visual differences between the paper and mudded areas after painting.
